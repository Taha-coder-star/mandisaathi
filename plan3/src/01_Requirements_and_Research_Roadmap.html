Context

We are building a WhatsApp-first assistant for Pakistani farmers and small traders. It must:

  • answer price questions in Urdu, Roman Urdu, English and mixed text, and in Urdu/Punjabi-accented voice notes;
  • say what the price should be in nearby mandis over the next days, with an honest range (F1);
  • say where to sell: the mandi that pays the most after transport, commission and spoilage (F2);
  • say what the price is likely to be at harvest, 3–5 months ahead, before the farmer plants (F3);
  • send daily rates and price alerts the farmer asked for.

The goal for FYP-I (evaluation in the week of 21–23 Dec ASSUMPTION) is a convincing, honest demo plus first measured results. The goal for FYP-II (May 2027) is a deployed service, a sealed-lockbox evaluation and a user test. The architecture must serve both without a rewrite.

1. Product understanding (precise statement)

A conversational price-advice system with WhatsApp as the only user interface for farmers, and a batch analysis pipeline that produces every number the bot is allowed to say.

FlowInputSystem roleOutput

Short-term forecast (F1)"Agle hafte tamatar ka rate?"Read the stored daily forecast for that crop × mandiP10–P90 range for 1–7 days, reason in plain words

What it is NOT, at least initially: a trading platform, a buyer–seller marketplace, a farmer app, a general chatbot, or a replacement for Zarai Mandi or AMIS rate pages. The AI is the project's assistant and never claims to be a person or a government service.

2. Functional requirements

2a. CONFIRMED (stated by the team or the supervisor)

Farmer side

  • FR-F1 Receive farmer messages on WhatsApp; no app install, no login.
  • FR-F2 Understand English, Roman Urdu, Urdu script and code-mixed text, including informal spelling ("pyaz/piaz/payaz", "tamatar/timatar").
  • FR-F3 Accept Urdu voice notes as first-class input (treated exactly like typed text).
  • FR-F4 Accept a location pin as the farmer's origin.
  • FR-F5 Extract: intent, crop, place (mandi, city or district), quantity + unit, date or horizon.
  • FR-F6 Return the latest observed rate with its date and source.
  • FR-F7 Return a 1–7 day price forecast as a range, never a single promise (F1).
  • FR-F8 Rank mandis by net revenue after transport, commission and spoilage (F2).
  • FR-F9 Return a harvest-month outlook 3–5 months ahead with a caution line (F3).
  • FR-F10 Subscribe to daily rates and threshold alerts; STOP unsubscribes and deletes.
  • FR-F11 Reply in the farmer's language; numbers in the farmer's unit (usually per mann of 40 kg).

Data and analysis side

  • FR-D1 Load Zarai Mandi data (via the co-supervisor) and AMIS public data into one canonical price table. FYP-I uses team-generated synthetic data in the same schema (A1).
  • FR-D2 Daily ingest, quality check and scoring without manual steps.
  • FR-D3 Every reported result is reproducible from a commit, a parameter set and a data hash.
  • FR-D4 Final results are measured once on a sealed lockbox year.

2b. IMPLIED (follow from the confirmed ones; need sign-off)

  • An echo of what was understood ("Pyaz, Okara, 50 mann") so a wrong parse is visible and correctable with one tap.
  • One clarifying question when a critical slot is missing, never a guess (e.g. "Kaunsi fasal?").
  • A coverage rule: crops and mandis without enough data get an honest "is ka andaza nahi de sakte", not a forecast.
  • A freshness rule: if the latest rate is older than 2 days, the reply says so with the date.
  • Conversation memory per farmer: last crop, place, quantity, and the last list shown (so "2 number wali" works).
  • Consent on first contact: what is stored and how to stop.
  • Source acknowledgement in replies (AMIS rule 10 of the fetch policy; the same courtesy for Zarai Mandi).
  • An audit trail: which forecast run and which data produced each number sent.

2c. CANDIDATES (not requested; decide in or out)

  • Voice replies (text-to-speech) for farmers who cannot read.
  • "What should I plant?" comparison across crops (two F3 outlooks side by side).
  • Price reports from farmers ("aaj Okara mein 2,800 mila") as a crowd signal.
  • Weather advice, input prices, government support prices.
  • A web dashboard for farmers (contradicts the WhatsApp-only goal).
  • A read-only admin view for the team and jury (data freshness, accuracy, message log).

5. Farmer journey (target, demo level)

  1. Entry: the farmer messages the MandiSaathi number from a shared link, QR poster or contact. (Which number? Test number allows ~5 phones only; see R1.)
  2. Greeting, disclosure and consent: "Main MandiSaathi ka AI assistant hoon… aapka number aur fasal yaad rakhi jayegi. Band karne ke liye STOP likhein."
  3. Question: one message ("50 mann pyaz Okara, kahan bechoon?") or several turns. The bot normalises units (mann, kg, bori, ton → kg) and places (district, city, mandi, pin) and asks only about missing critical slots.
  4. Answer: deterministic facts rendered from tool results (rates, ranges, ranking) plus a one-line plain explanation.
  5. Refinement: "aur tamatar?", "Lahore ka kya?", "2 number wali mandi ki tafseel", "agle hafte?" update the state and re-run the tool.
  6. Subscription: "roz rate bhejo" / "2500 se upar ho to batana" → confirmation with buttons.
  7. Dormant: after 24 h of silence, the bot may write only with approved templates VERIFIED (Meta pricing doc; Manzil R5).

Edge cases to design for: greetings only; a crop we do not cover (cotton, rice); a place outside coverage (Sindh); "bori" without weight; Punjabi words; questions about pesticides or loans (out of scope); abuse; a trader fishing for bulk data; voice notes with tractor noise; forwarded audio.

6. Daily data journey (no user)

  1. 06:00 PKT: load yesterday's and today's published rates from each source; save raw files with hashes.
  2. Validate: schema, units, name matching to mandi and crop IDs, outlier flags, duplicate source rows.
  3. Build features under the information-set rule (only data reported before the issue day).
  4. Score F1 for every covered series; write P10/P50/P90 with a run_id. Monthly: score F3.
  5. Evaluate alerts against the new data; send digests and alerts.
  6. Write a data-health row; if anything failed, the bot keeps answering from the last good run and says its date.

Risks: the source publishes late or skips holidays; a mandi changes its name; a typo multiplies a rate by 10; the export has no "published at" time, which breaks the timing rule.

7. Data lifecycle

raw source file ─► raw store (hash, retrieved_at) ─► price_obs (canonical, outlier_flag)
     ─► features (as of issue day) ─► forecast / outlook (run_id, model_version)
     ─► reply facts (message_log keeps run_id + tool payload) ─► evaluation (live accuracy table)
  • Every price row keeps its source, its raw reference and the time we learned it (reported_at).
  • Forecast rows are never overwritten: a re-run gets a new run_id, so live accuracy can be measured later.
  • Farmer data: number (encrypted + hashed), language, crops followed, approximate district, subscriptions, message log. Retention policy is decision D16.
  • Voice audio: kept only until transcribed (benchmark recordings are team-made and kept).

8. Major technical challenges

  1. Roman Urdu / Punjabi-tinted farmer language: no standard spelling; local crop names; numbers as words ("sau mann", "dedh sau"). We have no evaluation set yet; we must build one.
  2. Units: mann/maund (40 kg), bori (50 kg or 100 kg, varies by crop and place), crate (tomato), dozen; sources report per 40 kg or per 100 kg. Wrong normalisation means wrong rupees.
  3. Place ambiguity: a city can have several mandis (Lahore: Badami Bagh and others BELIEF – VERIFY); district vs city names; spellings ("Sahiwal/Saiwal"); location pins far from any covered mandi.
  4. Hallucination control: the LLM must only say numbers that come from our tools, including number words in Urdu.
  5. Timing honesty: a forecast for day t may use only data reported before t. If the source has no publish time, we must assume a lag and test it.
  6. Thin and gappy series: Sundays, Eid, missing mandis; some crop × mandi series are too sparse to model.
  7. Long horizon, small sample: about 17 harvest seasons since 2007 per crop × region; skill claims must respect that.
  8. Cost parameters for F2: transport rate, commission, market fee and spoilage vary and are not published in one place.
  9. WhatsApp limits: 24 h window, templates for alerts, buttons ≤ 3, list ≤ 10 rows, test number ≤ 5 recipients VERIFIED / REPORTED (file 02).
  10. Voice: Urdu STT may output Urdu script, Hindi script or garbage on noise; silence can produce invented text (seen on 2026-10-06 with Gemini audio in the Manzil project).
  11. Data permission: Zarai Mandi data may be usable for the demo but not for published results.
  12. Novelty: a Namal FYP has the same headline scope; Zarai Mandi sells a "forecast" service. Our claims must rest on measured evidence.

9. Unknowns that require research

R-A: WhatsApp / Meta platform (gates the channel)

  • R1 Access path and numbers: direct Cloud API vs BSP vs Twilio sandbox; test-number recipient limit; a real number without business verification; messaging limits for alerts.
  • R2 Meta's AI-provider rule (§4.7) and the ban on training models with platform data: does a farmer price assistant qualify as "business-specific AI"? What does the data-use ban mean for our message test set and any parser training?
  • R9 Message formats and alert templates: buttons, lists, location request, template categories (is a user-requested price alert "utility" or "marketing"?), PK template prices, opt-in rules.

R-B: Data and domain

  • R3 Data sources and permission: Zarai Mandi export (format, mandis, crops, history length, publish time, licence to publish results); AMIS coverage since 2007; weather (Open-Meteo), CPI (PBS), diesel (OGRA), road distances (OSRM/OpenStreetMap).
  • R4 Units, crop names, grades and the mandi list: which mandis exist per city, their coordinates, Urdu and local names, how each source reports grades.
  • R7 F2 cost parameters: transport per km by vehicle size, commission, market fee, unloading, spoilage per day; from traders and the co-supervisor.
  • R13 Prior work and competitors: the Namal FYP, Zarai Mandi's paid forecast, AMIS app, Kissan helplines, published papers.
  • R14 User-test recruitment and ethics: who, how many, consent form, what we may record.

R-C: AI / technical

  • R5 Short-term forecasting: which baselines are hard to beat at 1–7 days for daily mandi prices; global gradient boosting vs per-series models; interval calibration.
  • R6 Long-term outlook: is there skill 3–5 months ahead over climatology; what inputs are known at issue time (area sown, last season's price).
  • R8 LLM and STT for farmer Urdu: rule-based parser vs LLM tool calling; which model at $0 or near $0; which STT handles Punjabi-accented Urdu voice notes; data-use terms of each vendor.
  • R10 Conversation state: what must persist for follow-ups ("aur tamatar?", "2 number wali").
  • R11 Hosting at $0 with a public HTTPS webhook for FYP-I and FYP-II.
  • R12 Pakistan data protection status and consent wording.

10. Assumptions we must NOT make

  • ✗ That the co-supervisor's Zarai Mandi data will arrive, on time, with history, or with permission to publish results.
  • ✗ That we may scrape zaraimandi.com or its app API (fetch policy rules 2 and 11 forbid it).
  • ✗ That a personal WhatsApp number or an unofficial library (whatsapp-web.js, Baileys) is acceptable. It breaks WhatsApp's terms and risks a ban.
  • ✗ That the Meta test number can serve 10–15 user-test farmers or let a jury member message from their own phone.
  • ✗ That we can message farmers freely after 24 h, or that daily digests are free.
  • ✗ That any AI chatbot is automatically allowed on WhatsApp Business.
  • ✗ That the LLM understands farmer Roman Urdu, Punjabi words or Urdu number words without testing.
  • ✗ That "mann" or "bori" means one fixed weight everywhere, or that every source reports per 40 kg.
  • ✗ That a city name identifies one mandi.
  • ✗ That the source's "market date" is the day we could have known the price.
  • ✗ That a model beats yesterday's price just because it is complex; or that F3 has any skill at all.
  • ✗ That the cheapest-looking far mandi is a good recommendation when the forecast difference is inside the noise.
  • ✗ That the LLM may state a price, range or ranking that a tool did not return.
  • ✗ That free-tier LLM/STT vendors do not keep or train on what we send them.
  • ✗ That real farmer messages may be used to train or tune any model (Meta §4.7).
  • ✗ That a jury will accept "same as Namal but on WhatsApp" as novelty.
